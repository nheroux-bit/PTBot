# EdTech Precedent Transaction Analysis — United States, CY2016

## Executive Summary

This report presents a precedent transaction analysis of five closed M&A transactions in the U.S. EdTech sector (broadly defined) during calendar year 2016. After deduplication and quality review, five unique deals were identified from the compiled research. Every included deal has at least one explicitly stated or reliably computable standard valuation multiple backed by primary-source financial data.

**Scope parameters:**
- Sector: EdTech (including adjacent professional education and scholarly publishing infrastructure)
- Geography: United States (target HQ)
- Date window: Announced CY2016 (Jan 1 – Dec 31, 2016)
- Minimum qualifying multiples per deal: 1

## Detailed Deal Profiles

### Deal 1: Apollo Education Group (University of Phoenix)

**Transaction summary:**
- **Target:** Apollo Education Group, Inc. (NASDAQ: APOL) — Phoenix, AZ
- **Acquirer:** Consortium — The Vistria Group, Apollo Global Management (NYSE: APO), Najafi Companies
- **Structure:** All-cash take-private
- **Deal value:** $1.14B ($10.00/share; originally $9.50, raised May 1, 2016)
- **Announced:** February 8, 2016 | **Closed:** February 1, 2017
- **Advisors:** Barclays, Credit Suisse, Evercore (to target); Paul Weiss (to consortium)

**Valuation multiples:**
- **EV/Revenue:** ~0.5× ($1.14B / ~$2.1B FY2016 net revenue)
  - Source: PIE News confirmed $2.1B FY2016 net revenue; deal value per SEC Exhibit 99.1

**Strategic rationale:** Turnaround/take-private thesis removing the company from public-market scrutiny during multi-year transformation. Consortium brought political/regulatory navigation expertise (Tony Miller, former DOE Deputy Secretary) and PE education-sector experience (Apollo Global: McGraw Hill Education, Connections Academy, Sylvan Learning). Target had declining enrollment (477K peak → ~130K) and revenue, FTC/AG investigations, and DOE probation.

**Key sources:**

### Deal 2: Higher One Holdings / CASHNet → Blackboard (Providence Equity Partners)

**Transaction summary:**
- **Target:** Higher One Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: ONE), d/b/a CASHNet — New Haven, CT
- **Acquirer:** Winchester Acquisition Corp. (subsidiary of Blackboard Inc. affiliate; Blackboard owned by Providence Equity Partners) — Washington, DC
- **Structure:** All-cash tender offer + short-form merger (Section 251(h) DGCL)
- **Deal value:** $260M equity ($5.15/share); ~$239M EV (continuing operations)
- **Announced:** June 30, 2016 | **Closed:** August 4, 2016
- **Advisors:** Raymond James (to target); Cleary Gottlieb (legal, to target)

**Valuation multiples:**
- **EV/Revenue (continuing ops):** ~2.9× ($239M EV / $83M CASHNet Payments FY2015 revenue)
- **EV/Adj. EBITDA (continuing ops):** ~10.8× ($239M EV / $22.1M Payments adj. EBITDA FY2015)
- **Market premium:** 37.3% over June 29, 2016 closing price of $3.75
  - Revenue source: Higher One Q1 2016 earnings release (Payments segment FY2015 revenue $83M); Washington Business Journal
  - Premium source: BusinessWire press release

**Strategic rationale:** Platform consolidation — CASHNet (market leader in higher-ed tuition payments, 700+ campuses) merged with Blackboard Transact to create dominant campus financial services suite. Part of new CEO Ballhaus's strategy to reposition Blackboard for potential sale. ~60 potential bidders were contacted; multiple rounds of competitive bidding.

**Key context:** Higher One had divested its Disbursements business (to Customers Bancorp, Jun 2016) and Data Analytics/Campus Labs (Nov 2015) prior to announcement; the remaining entity was exclusively CASHNet.

## Comparable Multiples Context

### EV/Revenue Multiple Distribution

The four deals with US-based acquirers produced a wide EV/Revenue range (0.5× to 8.3×), reflecting fundamentally different business models and growth profiles:

**High-growth, asset-light, recurring revenue (8.3×):**
ACAMS commanded the highest multiple due to its regulation-driven recurring certification revenue, 30%+ CAGR, <10% market penetration in a $2.2B+ TAM, and asset-light model. This represents a premium for "picks-and-shovels" businesses in regulated industries.

**Platform/SaaS infrastructure (2.9× – 3.9×):**
CASHNet (~2.9×) and Atypon (~3.9×) represent campus-commerce and scholarly-publishing SaaS platforms with strong institutional switching costs, recurring revenue characteristics, and market-leadership positions. Atypon's premium reflects its platform network effects (1/3 of English-language scholarly articles).

**Distressed for-profit education (0.5×):**
Apollo Education's deeply depressed multiple reflects secular enrollment decline (470K → 130K), intense regulatory overhang (FTC, California AG, DOE), and market skepticism about for-profit higher education. ISS described the price as "extraordinarily low valuation."

**Deeply distressed consumer EdTech hardware (0.09–0.11×):**
LeapFrog's near-zero EV/Revenue reflects a company in effective wind-down — revenue declining 40%+ YoY, deeply negative EBITDA, and a market segment disrupted by general-purpose tablets. The 75.4% market premium was meaningful only because the stock was already at distress levels ($0.57/share).

### EV/EBITDA Context

Only the CASHNet deal produced a reliable EV/EBITDA multiple (~10.8×). Apollo's EBITDA was depressed/declining, LeapFrog's was deeply negative, and ACAMS/Atypon EBITDA figures were not publicly disclosed at sufficient granularity.

### Benchmark Context

For reference, the broader EdTech M&A market in 2015-2017 (per Raymond James EdTech quarterly reports) showed:
- Campus payments/financial services: 2.5×–4.0× EV/Revenue was typical
- SaaS/platform EdTech: 3.0×–6.0× EV/Revenue for growing platforms
- Professional certification/training: 6.0×–10.0× for high-growth recurring models
- Distressed for-profit education: 0.3×–1.0× EV/Revenue

The five deals in this analysis are broadly consistent with these ranges.

## Key Takeaways for Valuation

1. **Business model is the primary driver of multiples dispersion.** The 0.5×-to-8.3× EV/Revenue range is almost entirely explained by growth profile, revenue recurrence, and regulatory risk — not by deal size or sector label. Asset-light certification businesses (ACAMS) command 15-80× the multiple of distressed for-profit universities (Apollo).

2. **Regulatory risk is a decisive discount factor.** The two most regulated deals (Apollo at 0.5×, Higher One at 2.9×) carried meaningful discounts vs. the two least-regulated deals (ACAMS at 8.3×, Atypon at 3.9×). Apollo's multiple was depressed by FTC investigation, California AG probe, DOD probation, and Title IV score deterioration. The lengthy regulatory close timeline (12 months for Apollo vs. 2-6 weeks for Atypon/LeapFrog/ACAMS) is an additional cost of capital consideration.

3. **Platform economics and switching costs justify premium multiples.** Both CASHNet and Atypon were acquired as platform consolidation plays where the acquirer was integrating a specialized tool into a broader ecosystem. CASHNet's 700+ campus relationships and Atypon's position hosting 1/3 of global English-language scholarly articles created durable competitive moats.

4. **Brand value persists even in distress.** LeapFrog's 75.4% market premium (and VTech's willingness to pay $72M for a money-losing company) demonstrates that brand value and IP in educational products retains acquisition currency even when financial performance has collapsed.

5. **Take-private transactions may produce below-market multiples.** Apollo's 0.5× EV/Revenue should not be taken as representative of the broader EdTech market — it reflects a unique combination of secular decline, regulatory risk, and limited buyer pool. As ISS noted, it was an "extraordinarily low valuation" driven by the absence of alternative bidders willing to accept regulatory exposure.

6. **Professional education/certification commands premium pricing.** ACAMS's 8.3× NTM revenue multiple aligns with broader PE trends of paying premium multiples for certification bodies with recurring revenue, regulatory tailwinds, and credential lock-in. The 3-year recertification cycle and B2B institutional demand provide unusual revenue visibility.

7. **For valuation benchmarking purposes:** A reasonable EV/Revenue range for a healthy, growing US EdTech company in 2016 was approximately **2.5×–4.5×**, with discounts for regulatory exposure and premiums for recurring/certification-based revenue models.
